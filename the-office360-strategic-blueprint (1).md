# The Office 360: Strategic Blueprint
## Books · Authors · Publishing · Knowledge · AI · Learning

**Prepared:** 23 September 2026 | **Status:** evidence-led strategy, not legal or financial advice

> **Scope note.** This is a strategic blueprint, not a promise that every proposed feature should be built. The central recommendation is to sequence the ambition: retain the current high-trust studio as the cash engine, then productize its proprietary knowledge and relationships into a focused discovery-and-services platform before attempting a full marketplace.

---

## 1. Executive decision

The largest opportunity is **not “become another Amazon.”** It is to become the trusted operating layer for under-discovered books and the people who make them: positioning, metadata, rights-aware discovery, author services, promotion, and practical AI literacy—especially for African/Nigerian and independent publishing.

### Recommended wedge

**Office 360 Books & Knowledge Exchange**

1. A structured catalogue of independently verified books, authors, publishers, services, guides and courses.
2. A high-intent **Find a Lost Book** request service.
3. Productized versions of the existing studio services.
4. A free editorial knowledge library and email products.
5. Later: author workspaces, publisher feeds, marketplace transactions and AI tools.

Avoid launching a multi-sided marketplace, DRM reader, social network, print warehouse and AI writing suite simultaneously. Those businesses have incompatible economics and operational burdens.

### Why this fits the evidence

The current site already has a differentiated proposition: analysis before activity, named six-stage methodology, fixed-scope deliverables, a real audit sample, no fabricated testimonials, and a small senior team. It says it serves nonfiction/technical authors, independent authors and small-to-mid publishers. That is a credible foundation. It should not be discarded for a generic “everything for everyone” portal.

The Nigerian opportunity is real but price-sensitive and distribution-constrained. One market estimate projects Nigeria’s 2025 consumer books revenue at US$26.61m and e-book revenue at US$2.19m, but these are third-party estimates, not audited facts; use directionally only. Smartphone access, local storytelling and digital formats create opportunity, while low ARPU, payments, logistics, rights and trust constrain it.

# 2. Current-site audit

## 2.1 What the site currently is

The site presents itself as a four-person Lisbon/New York studio, established 2021, for book strategy, web, automation and research. The homepage’s primary job is to qualify serious briefs, not to sell books or teach AI.

Observed information architecture:

- Homepage with positioning, “who we help,” problem, trust signals, five services, work, methodology, team, FAQ, pricing shapes and contact.
- Service detail pages: `/services/book-positioning`, `/services/discoverability`, `/services/author-authority`, `/services/launch-strategy`, `/services/analytics-reporting`.
- Work/case-pattern pages, methodology, team and a downloadable 13-page sample report.
- Contact is a detailed brief form plus email.
- No visible bookstore, catalogue, account system, course system, public search, community, publisher portal or author workspace in the crawlable content reviewed.

## 2.2 Strengths to preserve

| Strength | Why it matters | Product implication |
|---|---|---|
| Sharp anti-vanity positioning | Differentiates from agencies promising reach | Make evidence, baselines and “what did not change” a platform principle |
| Concrete deliverables | Makes an intangible service purchasable | Convert deliverables into templates, diagnostics and paid products |
| Real sample report | Strong trust signal without invented testimonials | Publish more anonymized artifacts and methodology notes |
| Named people and roles | Human accountability | Build expert profiles with credentials, authored notes and availability |
| Clear workflow | Reduces buyer uncertainty | Turn Discovery → Analysis → Strategy → Implementation → Measurement → Optimization into customer-facing workflow states |
| Honest limitations | Credibility with sophisticated buyers | Keep editorial corrections, source notes and rights statuses visible |
| Excellent copy/editorial identity | Memorable and non-generic | Extend the typographic system; do not replace it with SaaS sameness |

## 2.3 Major weaknesses and missed opportunities

| What is wrong | Why / who affected | Replace with | Timing and value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Homepage is studio-first, not destination-first | A reader, learner or author with a low-intent question cannot see a next step | Audience switcher: **Read / Write / Publish / Promote / Learn AI**; persistent global search | Immediate; expands addressable demand and improves qualification |
| Navigation is mostly implicit/sectional | Users cannot scan an information architecture or return to a known destination | Explicit header IA, breadcrumbs, footer sitemap and role-based landing pages | Immediate; reduces cognitive load and improves crawlability |
| No public catalogue or structured entities | Books, people and services cannot compound as search assets | Book, author, publisher, course, event and resource records with stable URLs | 30–90 days; creates defensible data asset |
| Reliance on email for conversion | High-touch email is good for enterprise work but weak for lower-ticket demand | Diagnostic lead magnets, request workflows, consultations and checkout for defined products | 30–90 days; improves conversion and lowers sales friction |

| No content engine | Current authority is not discoverable at scale for “how to publish,” “find a Nigerian book,” etc. | Editorial pillars, glossary, research notes and templates linked to services | Immediate; SEO and nurture flywheel |
| “Current status” text appears dynamic/odd | Manifest/status timestamp can look like internal operational data | Replace with stable availability statement and last-updated metadata | Immediate; professionalism/accessibility |

| Images/metadata/accessibility not fully verifiable in text crawl | Need actual Lighthouse, mobile and source audit | Test title/description, canonicals, OG, alt, contrast, keyboard, forms, Core Web Vitals | Immediate technical sprint |
| No visible product economics | Visitors cannot self-select between advisory, deliverable and full engagement | Comparison table, starting scope/pricing bands or “request quote” rationale | 30–60 days |

## 2.4 Technical audit conclusion

The crawl exposes an editorially strong brochure, not a platform foundation. It appears capable of supporting an information site, but the proposed ecosystem requires a new domain model, authentication, payments, search, rights records, file processing, fulfilment and moderation. Do not bolt those onto a static marketing build without a product architecture.

**Audit actions before committing to stack:** run Lighthouse on representative desktop/mobile pages; inspect robots.txt, sitemap.xml, HTTP headers, CMS/build fingerprint, analytics consent, form processing, image sizes, schema, 404s, redirects, canonical tags and indexation in Search Console. Verify each result rather than assuming from visual inspection.

# 3. Market and competitive synthesis

## 3.1 Competitive map

| Category | Users choose it for | Strength | Gap Office 360 can occupy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amazon/KDP | Scale, fulfilment, discoverability, publishing access | Transaction and distribution infrastructure | Independent/African curation, rights-aware service, human analysis |
| B&N/Kobo/Apple/Google Play | Retail access and digital reading | Consumer commerce and device ecosystems | Cross-format, local context, hard-to-find requests |
| Goodreads/StoryGraph | Social discovery and reading tracking | Large graph/community | Better verified metadata, African/local catalogue, direct author outcomes |
| IngramSpark/Draft2Digital/Lulu | Distribution/print workflows | Infrastructure | Strategic positioning, quality control, human services |
| Reedsy | Vetted publishing freelancers | Marketplace of specialists | Integrated book diagnosis → delivery → sale/promotion |
| Wattpad/Medium/Substack | Publishing and audience | Creation/distribution loops | Finished-book and professional publishing pathway |
| Audible/Spotify | Audio consumption | Audio delivery and subscription | Rights-cleared African audio supply and production guidance |
| Udemy/Coursera/Skillshare | Courses | Scale and learning mechanics | Publishing-specific, Africa-relevant AI literacy with artifacts |
| Canva/Notion/AI writing apps | Creation/productivity | Low-friction tools | Guided workflows, provenance, human review, publishing output |
| Internet Archive/Gutenberg | Access and preservation | Public-domain/archive reach | Licensed “lost book” investigation; do not imitate unauthorized digitization |

The competitive answer is therefore **workflow + trust + local/global curation**, not “more features.”

## 3.2 Trends and implications

- Direct-to-reader and email ownership matter because authors want audience relationships rather than only retailer dependence.
- POD and digital distribution reduce inventory risk but do not solve metadata, demand, quality or local delivery.
- Audio expands reach, but rights, narration quality and unit economics need explicit modelling.
- AI is useful for transformation of human-owned work—editing, classification, metadata, repurposing and research support—but creates provenance, privacy, accuracy and copyright risks.
- Search is moving from isolated keywords toward entity-rich, answerable information. Google’s official Book documentation supports Book/author/edition data feeds; Course markup requires a real course and at least three courses for eligibility in the course-list guidance [Google Book documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/book?hl=en) [Google Course documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/course).

Do not rely on weak trend blogs for market sizing. Obtain rights, sales and audience data from partners, platform dashboards and primary industry bodies before forecasting.

**Find better books. Build better books. Make knowledge useful.**

Supporting line: *A human-led platform for books, publishing, author growth and practical AI literacy—with special attention to independent and African voices.*

## Defensible differentiation

1. **Evidence over hype:** every service/product has a baseline, source trail, owner and outcome definition.
2. **Book graph with context:** not merely ISBNs—edition, language, country, rights status, themes, audience, contributor and availability.
3. **Lost-book concierge:** legitimate investigation rather than piracy or a generic “search the web” box.
4. **African-to-global bridge:** local payment, delivery and cultural metadata plus export-quality pages for diaspora/global readers.
5. **Human-led AI:** visible provenance and author control; no opaque “one-click book factory.”
6. **One operating path:** discovery → manuscript → publishing → promotion → learning, with services when software is insufficient.

# 5. Platform architecture and staged scope

## 5.1 Core entities

`User → Role(s) → Author/Publisher/Promoter/Institution`

`Work → Edition → Format → Offer → Order → Royalty`

`Article/Course/Prompt/Resource/Event → Topic → Author/Source`

`Lost-book request → Candidate → Rights case → Resolution`

Every record needs: owner, source, verification date, rights status, territory, language, accessibility, last updated, moderation status and audit history.

## 5.2 Recommended stack

**Phase 1:** keep the existing marketing site or rebuild its content layer; use a headless CMS for articles/resources/entities, Postgres for structured records, object storage/CDN for media, a hosted search index (Typesense/Algolia/OpenSearch), managed auth, email CRM, analytics and a payment gateway supporting Nigeria plus international cards/bank transfer. Use a modular monolith rather than microservices.

**Phase 2:** add commerce/order service, digital delivery with expiring signed URLs, royalty ledger, publisher CSV/API ingestion, fulfilment integrations and tax/refund workflows.

**Phase 3:** add workspace/job queue, document conversion, AI gateway with model routing, retrieval over user-owned files, prompt/version audit, human review and deletion controls.

**Non-negotiables:** tenant isolation, encryption, malware scanning, backups, audit logs, rate limits, role-based access, consent, export/delete, incident response, accessibility and no training on manuscripts by default.

Avoid building DRM, custom payment rails, warehouse software, recommendation ML and community moderation infrastructure in the MVP.

# 6. Bookstore strategy

## Recommendation: catalogue first, inventory second

### Operating alternatives

| Model | Start? | Advantage | Risk |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Affiliate/referral | Yes | Low inventory/rights burden | Low margin, weak customer ownership |
| Marketplace consignment | Pilot | Broad catalogue, author/publisher supply | Seller quality, refunds, royalty reconciliation |
| Direct digital | Pilot with licensed titles | Instant global delivery, good margin | Rights/DRM/support |
| POD via partners | Later | Long tail without inventory | Margin, delivery time/quality |
| Own warehouse | No initially | Control and local availability | Capital, stock, logistics, returns |

Start with a curated catalogue, affiliate links and licensed direct offers. Add consignment marketplace after metadata and rights operations work. Use third-party fulfilment/POD; only stock proven local titles or institutional orders.

## Book page wireframe

**Above fold:** cover, title/subtitle, author, format/price/availability, verified rights/edition badge, Buy/Request/Save, preview.

**Below:** positioning statement, description, TOC/sample, author, publisher, reviews with purchase verification, metadata, accessibility, related books, source/last verified, share/affiliate link.

**Search/filter:** title, author, publisher, ISBN, topic, genre, country, language, year, format, price, condition, availability, reading level, audience, rights state. Search “old Nigerian book about Lagos in the 1980s” using hybrid lexical + entity + semantic retrieval, then show why each candidate matched and invite correction.

**Commerce:** cart, local/international payment, guest checkout, delivery quote, downloadable receipt, signed expiring file URL, watermarking only where legally/technically justified, refunds and support. Never promise universal availability.

**Revenue:** retailer affiliate commission, marketplace take rate, direct digital margin, promoted placement (clearly labelled), membership, bundles, institutional orders, services.

# 7. Find a Lost Book

## Public promise

“We will help identify and legally locate hard-to-find books. We will not distribute unauthorized scans.”

### Submission form

Title; author; ISBN; publisher; approximate year; country; language; remembered subject/plot; cover/title-page photos; excerpt limited to identification; where seen; desired format; contact; consent to publish a redacted request; urgency.

### Workflow

```text
Submit → duplicate check → catalogue/ISBN/library search → candidate matches
      → community suggestions → bibliographic verification → rights triage
      → author/publisher/estate/library outreach → licensed source or used copy
      → authorized digitization/POD/licensed ebook → notify requester → archive record
```

Statuses: `received`, `needs information`, `candidate found`, `rights research`, `licensed`, `used-copy only`, `public domain`, `unable to verify`, `closed`.

### Rights rules

Digitize only with public-domain status or written authorization/license. Store title-page images as identification evidence with access controls and takedown process; do not accept full scans for public display by default. A physical copy does not automatically confer digital reproduction rights. The Internet Archive controlled-digital-lending litigation is a material warning: the Second Circuit affirmed that unauthorized full-book digitization/lending was not fair use in that case [Finnegan summary](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/blogs/incontestable/internet-archives-controlled-digital-lending-not-protected-by-fair-use-second-circuit-finds.html). Jurisdiction-specific counsel must review any library model.

Business model: free basic submission; paid research concierge; success fee only where lawful/transparent; rights-recovery service for estates/publishers; library/publisher partnerships; authorized POD licensing.

# 8. Write, publish and promote

## 8.1 Author workspace MVP

1. Create project: genre, audience, language, target format, rights owner.
2. Import DOCX/EPUB/PDF; extract text; preserve original; virus scan.
3. Chapter tree, version history, comments, export.
4. Editorial checks: grammar suggestions, consistency, repeated terms, reading level, citation checklist—not silent rewriting.
5. Metadata: title/subtitle, BISAC/local categories, keywords, description, TOC, author bio, rights and AI provenance.
6. Human review gates: editorial, legal/rights, cover, accessibility, proof, retailer preview.
7. Export EPUB/PDF/print-ready package; distribute through selected partners or Office 360 marketplace.
8. Promote: media kit, launch plan, events, newsletter, affiliate links, campaign analytics.

### AI boundary

- **Assistance:** brainstorming, outline alternatives, error finding, classification, transformation, suggestions; author accepts/rejects.
- **Generated:** model creates substantive text, image or translation used in the book; record tool/model/date/prompt scope and disclose where required.
- **Human-authored:** author-originated text remains source of truth; AI never silently overwrites it.

Amazon’s current distinction is operationally important: AI-generated text/images/translations are disclosed in KDP, while AI assistance such as editing/brainstorming is treated differently. Re-check official KDP policy at launch; third-party summaries can lag. Platform rules do not resolve copyright ownership or infringement.

## 8.2 Publisher portal

Catalog import; validation; edition/ISBN management; covers/samples; territories/languages/rights windows; pricing; authors; inventory/fulfilment; promotions; featured submissions; sales/royalty reports; takedown; audit log. Charge subscription + transaction fee, with services and institutional packages.

## 8.3 Promoter centre

Campaign brief → eligible books → promoter applies → author approves rights/assets → unique tracking link/code → content/event → report → payment. Participants: reviewers, book clubs, schools, churches, universities, podcasts, creators and media. Require disclosure, anti-fraud, review integrity, age/privacy safeguards and opt-in contact permissions.

# 9. AI Academy and knowledge library

## Learning ladder

1. AI Beginner: concepts, limits, privacy.
2. AI User: prompting, verification, workflows.
3. AI Professional: documents, analysis, meetings, automation.
4. AI Creator: text/image/video workflows and provenance.
5. AI Author: manuscript support, metadata, audio, promotion.
6. AI Business: use-case selection, ROI, governance.
7. AI Automation: APIs, tools, evaluation.
8. AI Agents: retrieval, tools, permissions, monitoring.

Each course: outcome, instructor, short lessons, worked examples, practice file, quiz, capstone, rubric, sources, update date, certificate rules. Free introductions; paid cohorts, certificates, templates, corporate training and advisory. Do not sell a certificate as accreditation unless accredited.

## Prompt library record

`Title | outcome | user | inputs | prompt | example | variables | failure modes | privacy warning | rights/provenance | version | rating | author | last tested`

Allow save, fork, private/share, report, version history and ratings only after use; prevent prompts that solicit private data, impersonation, fraud, plagiarism or unlawful copying.

## Editorial scale without filler

Use a content brief with search intent, target reader, unique evidence, sources, expert reviewer, examples, internal links, update owner and acceptance criteria. AI may cluster queries, create outlines, compare metadata, transcribe interviews and draft alternatives; human editors verify claims and add original analysis. Every article shows author, reviewer, date, sources and correction path. No mass-generated location/keyword pages.

Content flywheel: one verified book → book page → review → interview → guide → newsletter → short video → discussion questions → event → promoter campaign. Rights-clear all quotations, covers, images and excerpts.

# 14. Trust, rights, privacy and safety

Establish a rights register per asset: owner, licence, territory, format, term, exclusivity, permitted excerpt, AI-training permission, takedown contact and evidence. Build notices/takedowns, counter-notice escalation, contributor warranties, review moderation, fraud controls and refund policy.

For Nigeria, obtain counsel on the Copyright Act/Nigerian Copyright Commission process, NDPA/NDPC obligations, cross-border transfers, VAT, consumer law and payment regulation. The Nigerian Data Protection Act framework includes rights, lawful bases, breach and transfer considerations; use the NDPC as the authoritative source rather than summaries. Internationally, GDPR/UK/US and local copyright rules vary. This report is not legal advice.

Privacy defaults: manuscript content not used for model training; explicit opt-in for product improvement; delete/export; retention schedule; child-safety controls; age-appropriate community; no public location or private sales data; human appeal for moderation and automated decisions.

# 16. Community, retention and analytics

Launch discussion and book clubs only after identity, reporting, moderation queues, rate limits, block/mute, spoiler controls, consent and human appeal exist. Start with moderated events and comments on editorial pages; delay open forums.

Email preference centre: New books, African books, author spotlight, writing, publishing, AI, rare discoveries, events. Automations: welcome/role selection, abandoned request, course progress, buyer receipt/follow-up, saved-book alert, author onboarding, inactive-user reactivation. Never force one newsletter.

Dashboard: acquisition source, search success/no-result rate, requests, catalogue growth/verification, conversion, gross margin, royalties due/paid, course completion, retained authors/publishers, moderation incidents, support time, LTV/CAC when data is sufficient.

# 19. Final recommendation

Build the **trusted discovery-and-workflow layer first**. The current studio has something most marketplaces lack: judgment, restraint, research discipline and an honest operating model. Preserve that advantage while converting it into structured public value.

The smallest valuable MVP is:

1. Role-based IA and universal search.
2. Verified book/author/publisher catalogue.
3. Find a Lost Book intake + human investigation workflow.
4. Five productized author/publisher services.
5. Ten authoritative content hubs and one newsletter preference centre.
6. Two practical AI courses and a rights-aware prompt library.
7. Measurement, consent, accessibility and rights controls from day one.

Only after these produce repeated searches, submissions, paid engagements, book transactions and course completion should The Office 360 build a full bookstore, marketplace, social community or AI agent layer. The strategic goal is not maximum feature count. It is to own a high-trust network where **books become discoverable, authors become capable, publishers become measurable, and AI becomes useful without becoming careless**.
